Move goldCarried->hero.gold/runGold and lootBag->hero.satchel; add killHero/wipe gold-disposition; add satchel weight to derive.carryUsed; write the v1->v2 migration. Re-baseline both goldens (state shape changes). Rationale: every later feature is then written against the final hero shape — no double migration.
2. RACES (racial traits only, ~6-8 races, NO personality-bias pool yet). Add hero.raceId + RaceDef JSON content + the derive() race-trait StatMod pass + the handful of bespoke racial hooks. Insert the rng.village race-pick in makeHero at a FROZEN position. Re-baseline goldens. Rationale: races are the second foundation that morale and religion both read; lock the hero identity model before the cross-cutting systems.
3. OWNERSHIP TRAITS minimal (greedy cross-hero-spend block, no rng). Thin guard layer on step-1 gold. Defer lone_wolf/gambling rng rolls. No golden churn beyond the block logic. Rationale: completes the economy foundation cheaply.
4. EVENTS (6-8 'any room' events + the pendingEvent pause/auto-resolve machinery). Self-contained: adds run.pendingEvent, the dungeonEvents slot in advanceTick, the EVENT_CHOICE command, deadline auto-resolve. Pins its rng draws (ephemeral hash2 for selection, rng.combat/loot for outcomes) in a fixed slot. Re-baseline goldens. Rationale: introduces run.favor/run.morale single-number scaffolding the next two systems can build on, and is mostly additive/leaf.

This is the first cross-cutting system; it reads downs/rescues/kills/loot already emitted. Define its EXACT advanceTick slot and rng.combat ordering NOW because step 6 must slot around it. Add a purity-grep test that applyMorale is the only morale mutation site. Re-baseline goldens. Defer 'recovering' gate + tavern.
6. RELIGION (favor/tiers/devotions/taboos/blessings + MINOR wrath only; NO avatars, NO negative-gold gods). Reads gold (step1), races (step2), morale (step5 — faith can modify morale). Slot its rng.combat devotion/wrath rolls in a FIXED position relative to morale's draws. Validate avatar defId / taboo families / devotion event-kinds at gen time like monster drop refs. Re-baseline goldens. Rationale: religion is the most eventful cross-cutting system and wants gold+race+morale stable underneath it.
7. NECROMANCER REWORK + SUMMONER (corpse-budget vs mana-menagerie). Self-contained caster work with deterministic FIFO/level/summon-order tie-breaks; ensure summon-order counter survives serialize. 8. PRIEST + GRAVE ROBBER + GAMBLER classes. Priest's Faith meter and Grave Robber's greed stacks read per-hero gold (step1) and religion (step6); Gambler's luck coins are self-contained rng.combat. 9. COVE GAMES + DARES (player-action gambling, NO idle addiction). Command/event-boundary rng.village rolls — determinism-safe because they're explicit actions, not idle accrual. Dare win-condition checks read the existing event stream (descend/potionUsed/monsterDied/runEnded/goldKept). Re-baseline goldens. Rationale: depends on per-hero gold and the Gambler class; lands before the deferred idle-addiction work.
10. (DEFERRED to a later batch, gated on a closed-form solution) gambling-addiction idle auto-gamble, manifestation avatars, mechanical loot arguments, negative-gold gods, race personality-bias pool, morale village levers, and the remaining ~12 events. Each is individually shippable later once its determinism/balance hazard is resolved; none block the v1 spine. Run `bun run balance` after steps 4,5,6,8 to confirm depth ~4-5 and ZERO timeout rows (panic-flee and event-pause are the two new movement-deadlock canaries).

## What to cut/defer for v1
> ⚠️ **Historical (planning phase).** This was the v1 scoping call. Since then, the deferred
> items were revisited and **most shipped** — gambling addiction, lone-wolf, manifestation
> avatars, and all four new classes are live. See the Decisions log above for the current
> status of each. The text below is preserved as the original reasoning.

- CUT gambling addiction from the idle/village path for v1 (it's the determinism landmine). Ship the Gambler CLASS and the Cove GAMES/DARES (which are explicit player actions at tick/command boundaries, determinism-safe) but defer the auto-gamble-while-idle 'addicted' trait until the per-minute-vs-offline-collapse problem has a closed-form solution. This removes the single highest-risk item with minimal player-facing loss.
- CUT manifestation avatars (the boss-summon wrath) from v1 of religion. Ship favor, tiers, devotions, taboos, blessings, and MINOR (Scorned) wrath only. Avatars are a party-wide wipe spawned from one hero's idle bookkeeping — high blast radius, lots of new boss-stat content and spawn logic, and the hardest to balance. Add them later once favor drift/floors are tuned.
- CUT the MECHANICAL loot-argument (who-gets-the-item via rng.loot) — ship only the cosmetic banter version (app-side, zero golden impact) as the architecture doc recommends.
- CUT debt_god's negative-gold balloon payment and compound interest, and money_god's fool's-gold, for v1 — they break gold>=0 invariants and per-hero accounting. Ship those two gods with only their non-negative-gold effects, or defer both gods entirely.
- TRIM the race roster for v1 from 14 to ~6-8 (human, dwarf, elf, halfling, half_orc, goblin, plus one weird one like skeleton). Each race adds racialTrait sim-hooks (regen, phase, fear, distributed-body damage-split, brittle-bones weapon-family, fragile-china once-per-run) that are bespoke special-cases like injuries — 14 of them is a lot of new sim surface. The exotic ones (swarm quorum wasted-action, ghost phase, porcelain crack) are the most bespoke; defer.
- DEFER race-personality-bias 'without replacement weighted pool.' For v1, keep the EXISTING rollTraits and just APPEND fixed racialTraits. The biased personality pool is pure flavor polish and adds a new weighted-draw codepath; the racial traits alone already deliver 'race sets identity.'
- CUT morale's village 'recovering' activity gate and the TAVERN_ROUND command for v1 (the doc already marks these as future). Ship morale's combat-mod bands + panic + the single applyMorale funnel; defer the village-economy morale levers.
- TRIM events to ~6-8 of the 20 for v1, favoring the ones that don't need new floor-feature preconditions (strange_tomb, mysterious_shrine, wishing_well, gambling_imp, friendly_chicken work with 'any room'). DEFER events needing new map analysis: cursed_idol_fork (roomGraph degree>=2), talking_door / chained_chest (block/consume specific tiles — these need partyLogic travel-gating that's riskier).
- DEFER the ownership-traits gambling_addiction siphon-at-embark and lone_wolf refusal-roll; ship greedy as a simple cross-hero-spend BLOCK (no rng) so per-hero gold lands without new rng draws in v1.

## Cross-system conflicts to resolve
> ⚠️ **Historical (planning phase) — many of these were resolved in the build.** Notably the
> "FATAL" gambling-addiction-vs-villageUpkeep conflict below was solved: idle auto-gamble
> accrues like chores (a fractional ms carry crossing a fixed interval, one seeded
> `rng.village` bet at a time), and since offline progress was removed entirely, the
> "one big offline call vs many online calls" divergence can't arise — closed time never
> accrues. The gold model was unified on per-hero gold; the negative-gold gods were not
> built; the three "cheat death" procs question was sidestepped (only Divine Intervention +
> the favor-scaled resurrection ship). Kept verbatim as the pre-build analysis.

- GAMBLING ADDICTION vs villageUpkeep purity (FATAL). The addiction mechanic says 'reuse the existing wall-clock chore-accrual cadence (~once per real minute), each idle addicted hero rolls from rng.village.' But villageUpkeep (src/game/run/runtime.ts:179) is explicitly Zero-RNG and is the SAME analytic function the offline catch-up path calls exactly ONCE to collapse up to OFFLINE_CAP_MS (12h) of elapsed time into one diff (src/game/village/offline.ts:34). It does not iterate minute-by-minute. So 'one roll per idle minute' cannot ride this path: either offline players gamble zero times (the loop never fires per-minute) or you must add a deterministic minute-iteration loop inside villageUpkeep that draws rng.village N times for N elapsed minutes — which makes the offline catch-up no longer analytic and changes its cost from O(1) to O(minutes). This is the single biggest architectural conflict in the batch.
- PER-HERO GOLD is a hard prerequisite that does NOT yet exist, yet gambling, money_god/debt_god religions, the Gambler/Grave-Robber classes' selfish gold siphons (Pocket It, Smash and Grab), and the dare 'goldKept' checks all assume it. Today gold is run.party.goldCarried + village.gold (types.ts:491,518); Hero has no gold field. Religions reference 'goldCarried' (debt_god debt servicing 'every 1200 ticks the party owes 8% of goldCarried'; trickster_god 'steal goldCarried per hit'; money_god avatar 'dmg scales with goldCarried') as a PARTY value, while gambling/classes assume PER-HERO gold. These two gold models are in direct tension: debt_god/money_god/trickster_god wrath are written against party goldCarried, but the architecture doc's Option A deletes party.goldCarried entirely. Pick one model before any of these ship.
- RELIGION taboos vs CLASS dependencies. sun_god forbids summons/skeletons ('-8 favor to cast a summon yourself', '-3 favor/300 ticks standing near a friendly skeleton') but the reworked Necromancer and the new Summoner are summon-identity classes; a Necromancer/Summoner who converts to sun_god is self-sabotaging by design — fine as flavor, but death_god+necromancer and sun_god+(summoner/necromancer) are mutually exclusive build axes that must be surfaced in UI or players will create broken heroes. Also poultry_god 'MUST NOT harm chickens' directly inverts the chicken_killer dare and the Fowl Play dare, and a hero could hold both a poultry_god vow and the chicken_killer dare simultaneously — the religion taboo and the dare's win-condition contradict.
- RELIGION wrath/blessing effects overlap with RACE traits and create double-immunity / double-dipping. e.g. poultry_god makes 'Heroes with Fear of Chickens immune to its panic' while Dwarf ale_courage and Skeleton/Golem/Lizardfolk already grant fear immunity — redundant but harmless. More serious: death_god multiplies permadeathChanceOnWipe by 0.7 AND money_god/various blessings alter revive; Porcelain Doll's fragile_china once-per-run survival, Halfling lucky_feet once-per-run lethal-whiff, and Divine Intervention resurrection all stack into the SAME 'would be downed' moment with no defined ordering. Three separate 'cheat death once' mechanics firing on one down event need an explicit precedence or they will interact unpredictably.
- MORALE design assumes races+religion DON'T exist yet and specifies them as 'the first consumer,' but this batch ships its OWN full races+religions designs that are far richer (favor tiers, 14 races with statMods). The morale doc's moraleProfile race shape and ReligionDef moraleHooks are a DIFFERENT, simpler schema than the races/religions designs in this same batch. Building morale against the morale-doc's invented race/religion schema will collide with the actual race/religion content schema. They must be reconciled into ONE race def and ONE religion def consumed by both morale and faith.
- OWNERSHIP TRAITS (greedy/lone_wolf) vs religion gold devotions vs the village store. money_god/debt_god 'gold devotions count double' and tithing pull from a gold pool; a greedy hero's gold 'can't buy for others'; the architecture doc routes shared services through the treasury but gear through per-hero gold. If a hero's personal gold funds their own tithe but the treasury funds shared services, then debt_god's party-wide '8% of goldCarried owed' has no single pool to draw from once gold is per-hero — whose gold services the party debt? Undefined.
- DARE auto-strip/auto-unequip (dare_bare_knuckle, dare_streaker, dare_naked_and_afraid) vs lone_wolf 'resists unequip via a roll' and vs Golem/Barbarian armorKinds. A dare that force-unequips at embark collides with lone_wolf's refusal roll (does the dare override the trait?) and is a no-op/free-win for races that already wear no armor (the design admits barbarians get dare_streaker free, but Golem can't use potions so dare_naked_and_afraid's 'set potions to 0' is also free for Golem — these should be gated or flagged).
- EVENTS that grant 'blessed'/'enraged'/spawn allies vs MORALE's social-beat scheduler vs RELIGION devotions all write to overlapping state on the same ticks. An event resolving via rng.combat at a tick boundary, a morale social beat firing every MORALE_BEAT_TICKS via rng.combat, and a religion devotion/wrath roll via rng.combat can all draw from rng.combat in the same tick — the ORDER of these three new draws relative to each other and to existing combat draws is unspecified and will determine the golden hash. Whoever builds second/third must pin a fixed ordering.

## Determinism risks
- FATAL: gambling addiction routes per-minute rng.village rolls through villageUpkeep, which is the analytic Zero-RNG offline catch-up path (runtime.ts:179, offline.ts collapses up to 12h into ONE call). A single villageUpkeep call for a 3-hour gap cannot reproduce 180 distinct per-minute gamble rolls without an internal deterministic minute-loop drawing rng.village 180 times — which destroys the O(1) analytic property AND means an online session (many small villageUpkeep calls) and an offline session (one big call) would consume the rng.village stream a DIFFERENT number of times for the same wall-clock, breaking save/load determinism. This must be redesigned: either gamble on a fixed integer-tick cadence inside the dungeon-style loop, or make the per-minute loop a pure function of (nowMs floor to minute, hero, seed) that yields identical draws whether called in one chunk or many.
- Three new systems all draw from rng.combat at tick boundaries with no defined ordering: morale social-beat scheduler (every MORALE_BEAT_TICKS), morale dodge/freeze (conditional draws), religion devotion/wrath/manifestation rolls (per-tick/per-200-tick/per-event), and event outcome resolution. The morale doc cleverly makes dodge/freeze CONDITIONAL (only draw when in the relevant band) to keep Steady parties stream-identical — but religion manifestation rolls 'every 200 ticks while any hero Forsaken' and morale beats 'every 100 ticks' are UNCONDITIONAL periodic draws that will shift the stream for ALL parties. Each system must define its exact slot in advanceTick relative to the others, and they must be built in a fixed order with goldens re-baselined per commit.
- Religion passive drift uses BOTH dungeon integer ticks (1pt/600 ticks) AND village wall-clock ('1 point per 5 min wall-clock, computed from nowMs deltas'). The village wall-clock drift has the SAME offline-collapse hazard as gambling: villageUpkeep must apply favor drift as an analytic function of dtMs, not a loop, or online vs offline favor will diverge. The design says 'computed from nowMs deltas, never read live' which is the right instinct but must be a closed-form clamp like the HP recovery, not iterative.
- Event eligibility uses an ephemeral rng makeRng(hash2(runSeed, 0xE7E7 ^ depth*131+ri)) keyed by (seed,depth,room) — this is the correct pure-function pattern (matches floor gen). But the OUTCOME resolution draws from the canonical rng.combat/rng.loot 'at the tick the choice is applied.' Since the player's choice timing is a Command applied at a tick boundary, and the auto-resolve fires at an absolute deadlineTick, the design correctly claims idle-replay == interactive-replay. The risk: the EVENT_TIMEOUT auto-resolve must fire at a tick that is itself deterministic and not dependent on when the modal was opened in wall-clock — verify deadlineTick = state.tick + CONST (it is) and that opening the modal doesn't itself consume rng.
- makeHero rolls raceId via pickWeighted(rng.village, RACES) BEFORE personality traits and name. This INSERTS a new rng.village draw before two existing draws, shifting every subsequent village-stream consumer (names, future hires, chores). Correct call by the design (re-baseline once), but every existing test that pins hero creation order breaks, and the ORDER of race-pick vs trait-pick vs name-pick must be frozen forever or all goldens churn again.
- Necromancer corpse-pool reanimation and Summoner menagerie cap both claim deterministic tie-breaks (FIFO order, highest-level-first, summon-order id) with rng.combat only for variant/target. Good — but 'menagerie_unleashed' expiring 'highest minion-level first, then highest summon-order id' when the window ends must use a STABLE summon-order counter that survives serialize/load. If summon-order is derived from insertion order in an array that serialize reorders, the tie-break becomes non-deterministic across load.
- Loot arguments: the architecture doc correctly recommends the COSMETIC version live app-side in banter.ts (Math.random, never touches GameState) — that is determinism-safe. The MECHANICAL version (rng.loot decides who gets the item) moves the golden and must be isolated. Risk is a future editor 'promoting' the cosmetic version into the sim without realizing it pierces the purity boundary. Document the boundary loudly.
- Morale persists between runs and is NOT reset at embark (by design). That's fine for determinism IF morale only ever changes via the single applyMorale funnel inside src/game. The risk is the 'cheer/jeer banter beat': the design splits it correctly (sim emits moraleBeat + applies delta; banter.ts only reads it), but if any morale delta is ever applied app-side in response to a banter line, the sim diverges from its own golden. The one-funnel rule (applyMorale is the only mutation site) must be enforced by a purity-style grep test.

## Balance risks
- RELIGION net power is wildly positive, not zero-sum like races. nonBeliever is explicitly the 'balanced' baseline (+5% XP, no strings), but every religion stacks large always-on bonuses (war_god +8-15% dmg, money_god +30% gold, glutton_god Well-Fed +20% dmg, moon_god full-moon +15% all stats) for the cost of avoidable taboos. A player who picks the religion matching their build and never violates taboos gets a strict, large upgrade over the faithless +5% XP. Favor tiers mean the bonuses are gated, but Exalted is reachable and parkable (drift is only 1pt/600 ticks). Religions need a real opportunity cost or everyone converts.
- Multiple 'cheat death' procs compress permadeath risk to near zero for a tuned party. death_god (permadeath x0.7 + 30% graveyard recovery + 'Not Yet' highest revive rate), Halfling lucky_feet, Porcelain Doll fragile_china, Priest Divine Intervention, and per-religion Divine Intervention all reduce wipe stakes. CLAUDE.md balance target wants 'a healthy mix of withdrawn and wiped' — stacking these will collapse the wiped rate and flatten the risk curve the whole game is built on.
- Gold inflation: money_god +30%, debt_god compound interest +1%/600 ticks, glutton/Greedy synergies, Elf/Halfling/Goblin/Pebble-Giant +gold racial traits, Grave Robber party-wide greed stacks, Money-god Midas tithe. Several of these multiply (mult gold stacks). A Halfling(+15%) + Loot Goblin context + money_god(+30%) + Grave Robber greed could push gold mult well past 2x, breaking shop/hospital pricing and the gambling stakes (which scale off wallet). The balance harness expectation of depth ~4-5 in ~10min assumes today's gold curve.
- Manifestation avatars are boss-statted and attack the WHOLE party but only trigger from ONE hero's Forsaken favor. A single mismanaged believer can spawn a boss that wipes a party that did nothing wrong. With 14 races, multiple religions, and favor drift, reaching Forsaken is plausible during a long idle session — this is a party-wide wipe risk attached to one hero's bookkeeping. Needs a floor on how bad favor can get while idle, or avatars become a feels-bad random wipe.
- Race personality-bias 'without replacement until count filled' with clamp-to-1 floor plus 1-3 personality traits on TOP of 1-2 racial traits gives 2-5 traits/hero. Trait delta-multipliers in morale MULTIPLY together (cowardly x1.4 loss x boastful x1.3 = x1.82); a few unlucky trait combos produce heroes who panic almost instantly or never. The morale doc embraces this ('delightful and emergent') but it widens outcome variance right when 14 races + religion already widen it — the balance harness wiped/withdrawn mix could swing hard.
- Gambler class and gambling-addiction idle losses both assume ~8% house edge keeps drift small, but the dares offer 5.7x-10x payouts and Skullcup/Wheel/Rat-Race have jackpot tails. A degenerate addicted hero is bounded by the RESERVE=25 floor and per-tick cap, which is good — but the per-day stage-gated loss plus variance means save-scumming or seed-farming a jackpot is possible if the player can re-roll the village stream by reloading. Verify the village rng is not re-seedable by save/load in a way that lets jackpot farming.
- Event auto-resolve defaults are all 'cautious/leave-it,' so a fully-idle player NEVER takes the high-EV aggressive event outcomes (treasure, allies, blessings). That's correct for safety but means events are pure downside-avoidance when idle and the rich rewards only exist for active players — fine, but the morale/favor deltas baked into defaults (e.g. +1 morale on 'leave sealed') are the only idle effect, so events become near-noise for the idle core loop they're meant to enhance.
- debt_god 'Debt Deferred' balloon payment 'may go negative as compounding debt' — a negative gold value will break every gold-gated check (shop affordability, gambling MIN_WALLET, dare goldKept>=200) and any mult that assumes gold>=0. money_god 'fool's gold vanishes' and Goblin 'loses item into its pockets' are net-economy washes on paper but feel-bad and interact with per-hero ownership accounting.

# Per-Hero Inventory & Gold — Architecture Design

This is the design for the per-hero inventory/gold refactor, the three ownership traits, loot arguments, and the 12-feature build order. I've read the actual sim, lifecycle, commands, hero create/derive, serialize, the loot/chest/pickup logic, the tactics evaluator, the banter director, and every read/write site of the four shared fields. Concrete symbols and line areas are cited throughout.

## 0. The single most important finding (it makes this tractable)

Loot and gold attribution is **already 90% solved in the data flow**. In `processDeaths` (`src/game/run/runtime.ts:1454–1481`) the killing hero is known via `m.lastHitBy` — that's where gold is currently credited to `run.party.goldCarried` and where drops are pushed to `run.groundLoot`. Both already have a hero id in hand. Pickup (`partyLogic`, `runtime.ts:1539`) already computes `taker` (the hero standing on the loot). Chests (`runtime.ts:1562–1580`) already compute `opener`. So "who got it" is never ambiguous — the data is on the floor, we just currently dump it into a party-shared bag. This is what makes the refactor mostly a *plumbing* change rather than a logic rewrite.

---

## 1. Exact type changes (`src/game/types.ts`)

### 1a. `Hero` — add a satchel and a purse

The hero needs a place to hold loot *during* a run (so it doesn't go to a shared bag) and persistent gold. Equipment, potions, and bombs are already per-hero (`Hero.equipment`, `Hero.potions`, `Hero.bombs` — lines 354–357), so they need no change.

```ts
export interface Hero {
  // ... existing ...
  /** gold this hero personally owns — never auto-shared (see ownership traits) */
  gold: number;
  /** unequipped items this hero personally owns (their pack/footlocker) */
  satchel: ItemInstance[];
  /** ownership/economy disposition flags, rolled at creation or via perks */
  ownership?: OwnershipTraits;
  // ... existing ...
}

export interface OwnershipTraits {
  /** won't let their gold buy gear for ANOTHER hero */
  greedy?: boolean;
  /** resists unequip/transfer of items they earned (rolls can fail) */
  loneWolf?: boolean;
  /** siphons a fraction of their gold at the Dungeon Gate into the "tables" */
  gamblingAddiction?: boolean;
}
```

**Design decision — do NOT overload the existing `traits: string[]`.** The current `traits` array is strictly banter-only (validated against `content/banter/traits.json` in `tools/gen-content.ts:269`, consumed only by `src/app/banter.ts`). The user's spec for `greedy`/`lone_wolf`/`gambling_addiction` requires *sim behavior* (gold can't cross-buy, transfers fail, gold leaks). Putting sim-affecting flags in a banter-validated string array would (a) couple the sim to banter content and (b) be fragile to JSON edits (`reconcileContent` drops unknown traits). So ownership lives in a typed `ownership?` object. Note: there is *already* a banter trait `greedy` (in `TRAIT_IDS`, `create.ts:34`) — the new `ownership.greedy` is a **separate, sim-affecting** flag. They can co-occur (a hero who *talks* greedy and *acts* greedy), or a hero can have one without the other. Keep the names distinct in code comments to avoid confusion; I'd suggest the ownership flag stay `ownership.greedy` and we lean on the banter trait for the talk.

### 1b. `PartyState` — keep `lootBag`/`goldCarried` as a *transient overflow*, not the source of truth

Two options:

- **Option A (clean, recommended): delete `goldCarried` and `lootBag` from `PartyState`.** Gold and items live on heroes the moment they're earned. The party bag disappears.
- **Option B (pragmatic): keep them but repurpose as an "orphan pile"** — loot/gold that belongs to a hero who is currently *downed and being carried* (can't carry their own pack) or for the rare ground-loot a *minion's* kill produced. On withdraw, the orphan pile is split.

I recommend **Option A** with one safety valve: when a hero is **downed**, new gold/items credited to them still go onto their `Hero.gold`/`Hero.satchel` (they own it even while unconscious — it rides home in their pack if they're carried). This is simpler than an orphan pile and matches the fiction ("you keep what's on your body"). The only true orphan case is a **kill credited to a hero who permadies mid-run** — but heroes only die at run-end (`endRunWiped`/`endRunWithdrawn`/`ABANDON_HERO`), never mid-tick, so the satchel always has a living owner at the moment of crediting. **`PartyState` loses both fields.**

### 1c. `VillageState` — `gold`/`inventory` stay, but their *meaning* shifts

`village.gold` and `village.inventory` remain (the village treasury and shared storage), because the player still needs a communal pool to buy from and a place to put items a returning hero hands in. **The change is at the boundary:** when a hero returns, their `satchel` and `gold` do NOT automatically dump into `village.*`. Instead the hero keeps them, and the player *chooses* to deposit (a new command). See §3.

This is the central product decision: "loot stays with the hero who got it." If withdrawn loot auto-merged to `village.inventory`, ownership would evaporate at the gate. So the hero keeps their satchel; the village inventory becomes "items explicitly deposited / bought into shared storage."

## 3. Village economy + the migration

### Who pays for gear bought for a specific hero
The user wants: *"a greedy hero's gold can't buy things for OTHERS."* Model:

1. **Treasury-funded shared services** (hire, potions, bombs, healing, training, rest) — paid from `village.gold` as today. Unchanged. These aren't "buying for a hero" in the ownership sense.
2. **Gear bought *for* a specific hero** at the Armorer: the player picks a hero and a payer. Default payer = treasury. If the player chooses to fund it from **Hero A's gold to equip Hero B**, the command checks: if A has `ownership.greedy`, **refuse** (emit a refusal + a banter line). A hero funding *their own* gear is always fine. Implementation: extend `BUY_STOCK_ITEM` with `payerId?: Id` and `forHeroId?: Id`; in the handler, if `payerId` is set, spend `roster[payerId].gold` instead of `village.gold`, with the greedy guard `if (payer.ownership?.greedy && forHeroId && forHeroId !== payerId) return`.
3. **Selling** from a hero's satchel credits **that hero's** gold (their loot, their money). Selling from village storage credits the treasury.

This keeps the common path (treasury) simple and makes ownership a *constraint layer* on cross-hero spending rather than a rewrite of the shop.

### Determinism golden
**The golden hash WILL break, deliberately, and that's expected per `CLAUDE.md`.** Reasons it breaks:
- Serialized state shape changes (heroes gain `gold`/`satchel`/`runGold`; party loses `goldCarried`/`lootBag`). `serialize(state)` text changes → `GOLDEN_STATE_HASH` (`determinism.test.ts:46`) changes.
- If you add **any new rng draw inside the sim** (gambling siphon on `rng.village`, lone-wolf refusal roll on `rng.village`, loot-argument tiebreak on a stream), the **event stream and downstream rng state shift**, breaking `GOLDEN_EVENT_HASH` too and cascading.

**Mitigation strategy for determinism:**
- Do the **pure plumbing rename first** (gold/satchel moves, no new rng) as its own commit. Re-baseline both constants in that commit (the test prints the new hash). The save/load-equivalence and "two identical games" tests (`determinism.test.ts:59,71`) must still pass — they will, since the change is deterministic.
- Add **rng-drawing features (lone-wolf refusal, gambling, argument tiebreaks) as separate commits**, each re-baselining the golden in the *same* commit. **Crucially: draw from the right stream.** Village-side ownership rolls (transfer refusal, gambling) → `rng.village`. In-dungeon loot arguments are **presentation only** and should live **app-side in `banter.ts` (Math.random)** so they don't perturb the sim at all — see §5. If an argument has a *mechanical* effect (e.g., a hero refuses to pick up an item another wants), that roll must be `rng.loot` or `rng.combat` and will move the golden.
- Keep new rng draws **out of the hot per-tick path** where possible — a refusal roll on `UNEQUIP` happens at a command boundary (rare), not every tick, minimizing golden churn.

### Migration (`serialize.ts`)
Bump `SAVE_VERSION` from 1 → 2 (`newGame.ts:8`). Add `MIGRATIONS[0]` (migrates v1→v2):

```ts
const MIGRATIONS = [
  (s: any) => {
    // v1 had party-shared loot/gold; v2 is per-hero.
    for (const id of s.rosterOrder) {
      const h = s.roster[id]; if (!h) continue;
      h.gold ??= 0;
      h.satchel ??= [];
      if (h.stats) h.stats.runGold ??= 0;
    }
    // a live v1 run had party.goldCarried + party.lootBag mid-dungeon.
    // Disperse to the standing members deterministically by rosterOrder so
    // an in-progress save doesn't lose its haul.
    if (s.run) {
      const std = s.run.party.memberIds.filter((m: string) => !(m in s.run.party.downed));
      const recipients = std.length ? std : s.run.party.memberIds;
      const g = s.run.party.goldCarried ?? 0;
      const per = Math.floor(g / Math.max(1, recipients.length));
      recipients.forEach((m: string, i: number) => {
        const h = s.roster[m]; if (!h) return;
        h.gold += per + (i === 0 ? g - per * recipients.length : 0); // remainder to first
        h.stats && (h.stats.runGold += per);
      });
      for (const it of s.run.party.lootBag ?? []) {
        const m = recipients[0]; s.roster[m]?.satchel.push(it); // simplest: first carrier
      }
      delete s.run.party.goldCarried;
      delete s.run.party.lootBag;
    }
    return s;
  },
];
```

This is deterministic (no rng), so save/load-equivalence holds across the migration boundary. The `reconcileContent` pass (which runs *after* migrations, `serialize.ts:55`) then validates satchel item refs.

## 5. Loot arguments (the "argue over loot" feature)

**Two tiers, pick per your appetite for sim impact:**

- **Cosmetic (recommended default, zero golden impact):** an argument is pure banter. In `partyLogic` pickup, when a *rare/epic* item is picked up and ≥2 standing heroes are within ~2 tiles, emit a **presentation-only** `lootArgument` event (or just let the banter director detect a `lootPicked` of a high-rarity item near multiple heroes). - **Mechanical (optional, moves the golden):** the argument changes *who gets the item*. When two heroes contest a tile, a `rng.loot` roll (weighted by greed/lone-wolf and level) decides the winner; the loser may get a brief `gloomy`-style debuff or nothing. This must use a sim rng stream and re-baseline the golden. Only do this if you want loot disputes to matter mechanically. The `greedy`/`lone_wolf` ownership flags make natural weights here.

I recommend shipping the **cosmetic** version first (it's free and on-theme with the whimsical banter system) and only promoting to mechanical if playtesting wants stakes.

## 6. Safe incremental build order for the whole 12-feature batch

Grouping the 12 features by dependency. **Foundational** features unlock others; **cross-cutting** ones touch many systems and should land on a stable base; **additive** ones are leaf changes.

**The dependency spine:** per-hero inventory/gold is foundational because ownership traits, loot arguments, gambling, and any per-hero economy all read/write `hero.gold`/`hero.satchel`. Races+traits are foundational because morale, religion, and trait-driven behavior all hang off the hero's trait/race model. Build these two first.

**Phase 1 — Foundations (must be first, each re-baselines the golden):**
1. **Per-hero inventory & gold (pure plumbing).** Types §1, all rename sites §2, migration §3, derive satchel weight. **No new rng.** Re-baseline golden. This is the load-bearing commit; everything else builds on it.
2. **Races + traits model.** Establish the typed trait/race container on `Hero` (the `ownership?` object from §1a is the first citizen of this). A `race` field + `RaceDef` content (JSON, like classes) and a typed trait registry that can carry sim-affecting flags. Foundational for morale/religion/behavior. Mostly data + `derive` mods; re-baseline golden (stat shape changes).

**Phase 2 — Ownership behaviors (depend on Phase 1, isolate rng):**
3. **`greedy` / `lone_wolf` ownership rules** — cross-hero spend guard (`BUY_STOCK_ITEM` payer), unequip/transfer refusal roll (`rng.village`, command-boundary, rare). Re-baseline golden in this commit (new rng draw).
4. **Loot arguments — cosmetic version** — app-side banter only (§5). **No golden change.** Can ship anytime after Phase 1's events exist; do it here while loot is fresh.
5. **`gambling_addiction`** — embark-time gold siphon (`rng.village`, `lifecycle.startRun`). Additive, re-baseline golden. Leaf feature; depends only on per-hero gold.

**Phase 3 — Cross-cutting systems (build last, on a stable base):**
6. **Morale** — a per-hero/per-party stat that reads many inputs (downs, rescues, loot disputes, gold, deaths) and feeds combat/banter. It's cross-cutting: it touches `processDeaths`, lifecycle, tactics, and banter. Land it *after* the economy and trait foundations exist so morale can read ownership/gold signals. Heavy golden churn.
7. **Religion** — per-hero faith/deity (content-driven like classes/races), grants buffs/quirks, interacts with morale and events. Cross-cutting (derive mods + events + village building). Build after morale so faith can modify morale.

**Phase 4 — Additive content/events (leaf, minimal coupling):**
8–12. **Events** (random dungeon/village events), and the remaining additive features. Events are additive: they're a dispatch/roll layer that *uses* the now-stable hero economy, morale, and religion as inputs/outputs. They don't require structural changes to the spine. Each event that draws rng re-baselines the golden, but they don't interact with each other, so order among them is free.

**Why this order:** Phase 1 changes the *shape* of `Hero`/`Party`/`Village` — doing it first means every later feature is written against the final shape (no double-migration). Ownership behaviors (Phase 2) are thin guards on Phase-1 fields. Morale/religion (Phase 3) are the expensive cross-cutting systems that want a stable economy + trait model underneath them — building them early would mean reworking them when the economy lands. Events (Phase 4) are leaves that consume everything below. **Re-baseline the two determinism constants in the same commit as each sim/content change**, and run `bun run balance` after Phases 2–4 to confirm depth ~4–5, healthy withdrawn/wiped mix, and **zero timeout rows** (the movement-deadlock canary).

**Key files to touch per phase:** Phase 1 = `types.ts`, `lifecycle.ts`, `runtime.ts` (processDeaths/partyLogic), `commands.ts`, `create.ts`, `derive.ts`, `serialize.ts`, `newGame.ts`, all UI views, all four affected tests. Phase 2 = `commands.ts` + `content/banter/pairs.json` + `app/banter.ts`. Phase 3+ = new content modules under `content/` and `src/game/content/`, new village buildings in `ui/village/`.

# The Morale System — Idle Dungeon Crawler

## 0. Design thesis & where it lives

Morale is a **per-hero integer** that the *simulation* owns, mutates, and serializes. It is the party's emotional weather, expressed as a number that combat outcomes push around and that combat behavior reads back. It threads three existing systems together: the **tactics evaluator** (panic gates an extra rule lane and suppresses aggression), the **combat resolver** (high morale buys crit/dodge/damage; low morale taxes them), and the **banter director** (which becomes a *reader* of morale, never a writer).

The hard rule from `CLAUDE.md` is non-negotiable and shapes the whole design:

> The sim is the single source of truth. `src/app/banter.ts` rolls `Math.random`, never touches `GameState`, and must stay golden-stable.

So the data flow is strictly one-directional:

```
SIM events (deterministic) ──► morale deltas (deterministic, in GameState) ──► combat/AI effects (deterministic)
                              └► GameEvent {t:'morale', ...} ──► app-side BanterDirector reads it as FLAVOR only
```

The banter director already consumes `kill`, `heroDowned`, `friendlyFire`, etc. It will gain a new event type to *react to* morale changes ("the bard cheers, +morale"), but the morale number itself is computed entirely inside `src/game`. Banter lines a hero *says* never move morale; only the **sim event that triggered the banter** moves it. A cheer is a sim event with a morale payload; the words layered on top are app-side decoration.

## 1. State, range, and baseline

Add one field to `Hero` in `types.ts`:

```ts
/** -100..+100, sim-owned. Persists between runs (decays toward baseline in village). */
morale: number;
```

- **Range:** clamped `[-100, +100]`.
- **Neutral baseline:** `0`. A freshly hired hero starts at `+10` (the optimism of a new contract — small, deliberate, so the tutorial party feels chipper, not flat). New field added to `emptyStats`-adjacent construction in `create.ts`; default `MORALE_START = 10`.
- **It is NOT reset at embark.** Morale is a persistent psychological trait — a hero who got traumatized last run starts the next one nervous. This is the whole emotional point. (Contrast with `hero.statuses`/`cooldowns`, which `startRun` wipes.) It only *recovers toward baseline* in the village over wall-clock time (Section 8).

All constants live in `balance.ts` under a new `// ---------- morale ----------` block. Every formula below is a constant or pure function there.

## 2. The threshold bands

Five bands. Boundaries are inclusive-low. A hero's *current band* is recomputed on demand (never stored) by a pure `moraleBand(m: number)` in `balance.ts`, exactly like `derive()` recomputes stats.

| Band | Range | Name | Feel |
|------|-------|------|------|
| **Panicked** | `[-100, -60)` | "Routed" | Breaks and runs |
| **Shaken** | `[-60, -25)` | "Rattled" | Timid, second-guesses |
| **Steady** | `[-25, +25]` | "Composed" | Neutral, no modifiers |
| **Confident** | `(+25, +60]` | "Emboldened" | Sharper, braver |
| **Inspired** | `(+60, +100]` | "Unstoppable" | Heroic |

The wide Steady band (a full 50 points) is intentional: most of the time morale is just *flavor texture*, and only the tails of the distribution flip mechanical behavior. This keeps the sim from feeling twitchy and keeps the determinism golden from churning on every minor scuffle.

## 3. What each band does — mechanically

Two pure functions in `balance.ts`, both keyed off the band, applied where the existing code already computes combat numbers and AI decisions.

### 3a. Combat modifiers — `moraleCombatMods(m)`

Returns `{ critAdd, dodgeChance, dmgMult, armorMult }`. These plug into `derive()` and the resolvers with **zero new RNG draws on the common path** except dodge (see the determinism note).

| Band | `critAdd` (additive to crit chance) | `dodge` (new: chance to fully avoid a hit) | `dmgMult` | `armorMult` (mitigation) |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| Panicked | **−0.05** | 0 | **×0.80** | **×0.85** (fumbling defense) |
| Shaken | −0.02 | 0 | ×0.92 | ×0.95 |
| Steady | 0 | 0 | ×1.00 | ×1.00 |
| Confident | **+0.04** | **+0.04** | ×1.08 | ×1.00 |
| Inspired | **+0.08** | **+0.08** | **×1.15** | ×1.05 |

**Integration points (surgical, matching the existing code):**

- **Crit:** `derive.ts` already computes `critChance: critChance(attrs.dex)`. Add the morale term there so it flows everywhere crit is read. Because `derive()` takes only `hero`, and `hero.morale` is on the hero, this is a one-line addition: `critChance: clamp01(critChance(attrs.dex) + moraleCombatMods(hero.morale).critAdd)`. The existing `chance(state.rng.combat, d.critChance)` calls in `resolveHeroAction`/`hitMonster` pick it up untouched.

- **Damage:** `d.dmgMult` already multiplies every hero damage line (`physDamage(...) * d.dmgMult`). Fold morale in: `dmgMult: dmgMultBase * moraleCombatMods(hero.morale).dmgMult`. One spot, propagates to attacks, physical skills, and offensive spells exactly as the existing `dmgMult` does.

- **Armor / mitigation:** in `resolveMonsterAttack` and `monsterSkillDamage`, the defender's `armor` is computed as `d.armor + (defending ? DEFEND_ARMOR_BONUS : 0)`. Multiply `d.armor` by `armorMult` so the Panicked tank actually feels squishier and the Inspired one shrugs hits. (Apply the mult to base armor only, not the defend bonus — defending is a discipline that morale shouldn't erode.)

- **Dodge (the one new RNG draw):** Confident/Inspired heroes get a flat chance to *fully negate* an incoming hit. This must draw from `state.rng.combat` to stay deterministic. Place it at the **top of `resolveMonsterAttack`** and `monsterSkillDamage`, right after the stealth check: if `chance(state.rng.combat, moraleCombatMods(hero.morale).dodge)`, emit a `{t:'damage', amount:0, dodged:true}`-style event (or a dedicated `{t:'dodge'}` event for fx) and return. **Critical determinism detail:** roll the dodge die *unconditionally* whenever a hero would be hit, even when `dodge === 0`, OR never roll it when `dodge === 0` — but pick one and pin it. The cheaper, golden-friendly choice: **only draw when `dodge > 0`**. That keeps the rng stream identical to today for any party that never reaches Confident, which means re-baselining the golden touches only seeds where morale actually crosses +25. Document this in the function's comment so a future editor doesn't "tidy" it into an unconditional roll.

### 3b. AI / behavior modifiers — `moraleBehavior(m)`

Returns `{ panic: boolean, timid: boolean, fearless: boolean }`. These hook into the **decision phase**, not the resolution phase, so they shape *what the hero chooses to do*.

- **Panicked (`panic: true`):** the hero's combat AI is overridden by a **flee impulse** before the tactics evaluator even runs. See Section 4 — this is the headline behavior.

- **Shaken (`timid: true`):** two soft effects in `heroDecisions`:
  1. **Miss-a-turn ("freeze"):** with probability `MORALE_FREEZE_CHANCE = 0.15`, drawn from `rng.combat`, a Shaken hero hesitates: it commits a `wait` action (the existing `{kind:'wait'}`, 5 ticks) instead of acting. Same determinism rule as dodge — only draw the freeze die when `timid` is true, so Steady+ parties never touch the stream.
  2. **Tactics softening:** see Section 5 — Shaken heroes get their offensive rules' *effective* aggressiveness reduced (they prefer `defend`/`retreat` lanes).

- **Confident:** no behavior override (their bonus is purely combat stats — they fight *better*, not *differently*). This keeps the AI legible.

- **Inspired (`fearless: true`):** immune to the auto-withdraw-on-downed *hesitation* — an Inspired hero will keep fighting one extra beat to cover a rescue (small, flavorful; implemented as: Inspired heroes don't get the timid freeze and ignore the "enemyLevelAbove makes me cautious" trait-panic in Section 6). They also **resist morale loss** (Section 7, a damping multiplier), modeling momentum.

## 4. Panic behavior — the centerpiece

When `moraleBehavior(hero.morale).panic` is true, the hero **routs**. This is injected at the very top of the per-hero loop in `heroDecisions`, *before* the built-in rescue logic and *before* `evaluate()`:

```
if panicking and enemies are engaged:
    if a hostile is adjacent → 'retreat' (the existing resolved action: step to the
        unoccupied neighbor farthest from the nearest enemy)
    else → flee toward the floor entrance via the existing entranceField flow step
    (refuse to advance, refuse to pick up loot, refuse to open chests)
```

Concretely it reuses machinery that already exists:
- `retreat` is already a `ResolvedAction` with a working resolver (`performResolved` case `'retreat'`).
- Fleeing toward the entrance reuses `entranceField(run)` + `flowStep` — the exact path the whole party uses during `withdraw`. A panicked hero behaves *as if personally withdrawing*, even while the party intends to explore.

Two guardrails so panic is dramatic but not a soft-lock:
1. **Panic never abandons the floor by itself.** A single panicked hero fleeing to the entrance just clusters there; it does not trigger `endRunWithdrawn` (that still requires *all standing* heroes regrouped at the entrance with `intent==='withdraw'`). So one coward sprinting for the door creates tension and split-party danger, exactly the desired drama, without ending the run.
2. **Panic is checked against engagement.** Out of combat (no engaged enemies), a panicked hero behaves normally for travel — they don't refuse to walk down empty halls; they just won't *fight*. This avoids the movement-deadlock/`timeout` rows the balance harness watches for.

**Refuse-to-advance** falls out for free: because panic short-circuits before `computeObjective`-driven travel during combat, the hero won't push toward the next room while routed. **Drop to a worse tactic** is modeled by Shaken's tactics-softening (Section 5), and **miss turns** by the freeze die (Section 3b).

## 5. Tying into the tactics evaluator

The evaluator (`evaluate`) is a pure function `(hero, d, view, set) → ResolvedAction | null`. Morale interacts with it in three clean ways that respect its "trigger-true-but-infeasible falls through" contract:

**(a) A panic pre-empt above the evaluator.** As in Section 4, panic is handled in `heroDecisions` *before* `evaluate()` is consulted. The player's rules are simply not reached while routed — the body has overruled the plan. This is the right altitude: tactics are deliberate strategy, panic is involuntary.

**(b) New morale triggers — player-authorable rules.** Extend the `Trigger` union so players can *program around* morale, which is squarely on-theme for a game whose signature mechanic is programmable tactics:

```ts
| { k: 'selfMoraleBelow'; value: number }   // "if I'm rattled, defend"
| { k: 'selfMoraleAbove'; value: number }   // "if I'm inspired, go all-in with the big spell"
| { k: 'allyPanicking' }                    // "if a friend breaks, cover them"
```

`triggerTrue` gains three cases reading `hero.morale` / scanning allies' morale via the view. To support `allyPanicking`, `BattleView` gains a parallel `alliesPanicking: Id[]` (built in `buildBattleView` from each ally's `moraleBehavior`). This lets a player write the cleric a rule: *"trigger: allyPanicking → action: cast Rally"* (a new support skill), making morale management an explicit strategic layer rather than invisible math.

**(c) Shaken softening as an implicit rule-reorder.** Rather than mutating the player's rule list (which would be confusing and break the editor's WYSIWYG), Shaken heroes get a **feasibility shim**: when `timid` is true, the evaluator treats offensive actions against *non-adjacent* enemies as infeasible — i.e., a Shaken hero will still defend itself if something is in its face, but won't *chase*. This reuses the exact pattern already in the code for `withdrawing` (look at `attackTarget`: while withdrawing, only adjacent enemies are feasible targets). We generalize that one predicate: `const passive = view.withdrawing || view.timid;` Then "drop to a worse tactic" emerges naturally — the aggressive top rules fall through to the defensive ones the player wrote lower down. No rule rewriting, fully deterministic, and it reads identically to the existing withdraw behavior players already understand.

## 6. Interaction with traits

Traits currently only flavor banter. Morale gives them *teeth* without breaking their banter role. Each interacting trait gets a tuning entry; traits not listed are morale-neutral.

Two mechanisms: **threshold shifts** (a trait moves the band boundaries *for that hero*) and **delta multipliers** (a trait scales specific gain/loss sources).

| Trait | Effect on morale |
|-------|------|
| **cowardly** | **Panics sooner & harder.** Band boundaries shift +15 in the negative direction *for panic/shaken only*: Panicked at `[-100, −45)`, Shaken at `[−45, −10)`. Also ×1.4 on all *loss* sources. The cowardly hero is the first to break — exactly as the prompt asks. |
| **bloodthirsty** | **Gains morale from kills** (×2.0 on the kill source, Section 7) and is *slower to break*: negative boundaries shift −15 (harder to panic). A bloodthirsty hero on a killing streak snowballs toward Inspired. |
| **boastful** | Bigger swings both ways (×1.3 on gains *and* losses) — dramatic, mercurial. Extra morale when complimented/cheered. |
| **gloomy** | Baseline pulled to **−10** (their personal "neutral"); village recovery still targets *their* baseline, so they idle perpetually a little down. ×0.7 on gain sources (hard to cheer up). |
| **cheerful** | Baseline pulled to **+10**; ×1.3 on cheer/compliment sources; ×0.8 on losses (resilient). The party's morale engine. |
| **deadpan** | Damped both directions (×0.6 all sources). The flat-affect hero is a morale anchor — barely moves, good for stability. |
| **superstitious** | Doubles morale loss from **god's-wrath** sources and from *omens* (a teammate's death, a missed turn streak). Religion-sensitive (Section 9). |
| **poetic / scholarly / motherly / hungry / greedy** | Banter-only for v1; reserved hooks (e.g., **motherly** could gain morale when *rescuing* an ally, **greedy** from looting — natural future extensions, left as content knobs). |

Implementation: a small `MORALE_TRAIT_MODS` table in `balance.ts`: `Record<traitId, { baseline?: number; gainMult?: number; lossMult?: number; bandShift?: number }>`. `moraleBand`, `moraleBaseline`, and the delta-application helper each fold in the hero's traits. Because a hero has 2–3 traits, multipliers **multiply together** and band shifts **sum** (a cowardly+boastful hero is a panicky drama queen — and that's delightful and emergent).

## 7. Every concrete gain/loss source, with values

All applied through **one funnel**: a pure `applyMorale(hero, base, source)` helper that (1) multiplies `base` by the hero's trait gain/loss mults and any `fearless` damping, (2) adds to `hero.morale`, (3) clamps to `[-100, 100]`, (4) emits `{ t: 'morale', heroId, delta, source, value }` for fx/banter. **Every** morale change goes through this funnel — there is exactly one mutation site, which keeps the golden hash auditable and the determinism contract trivially enforceable. The funnel is called *inside the sim*, at the same code sites that already emit the triggering event.

### Gains (positive base, before trait mults)

| Source | Base | Where it fires |
|--------|------|------|
| **Kill** (you landed the killing blow) | **+3** | `processDeaths`, on `m.lastHitBy` (bloodthirsty ×2 → +6) |
| **Boss/elite kill** (killing blow on boss) | **+12** boss / **+6** elite | `processDeaths`, boss/elite branch |
| **Witnessed kill** (a standing ally killed something) | **+1** to each other standing hero | `processDeaths`, broadcast to party |
| **Rescued a friend** (you picked up a downed ally) | **+4** to carrier | the `heroCarried` emit sites |
| **Got rescued** (carried to safety) | **+3** to the carried hero | same |
| **Cheered / complimented** (banter-driven, see below) | **+5** | new `Rally`/cheer skill *and* a sim-side "banter beat" hook |
| **Looted a chest** | **+2** to the opener, **+1** party | `partyLogic` chest branch (greedy ×1.5 future) |
| **Survived a boss floor** (descended past a boss) | **+5** party | `enterFloor` when crossing `depth % BOSS_EVERY === 1` |
| **Level up** | **+4** | `addXp` / `levelUp` event site |
| **Won a bet** (see religion/gambling shrine, §9) | **+6** | shrine/gamble resolution |
| **Full HP & no enemies for a stretch** (calm regroup) | **+1 / 10s capped** | `partyLogic`, gentle drift up toward baseline mid-run |

### Losses (negative base, before trait mults)

| Source | Base | Where it fires |
|--------|------|------|
| **Saw a friend fall** (an ally got `heroDowned`) | **−10** to every standing hero (the headline trauma) | `processDeaths` hero-down branch, broadcast |
| **You got downed** (recorded for the *next* run, since you're now out) | **−15** to self | same — sets morale low so they start next run shaken |
| **Friendly fire** (hit by an ally's bomb/AoE) | **−6** to victim, **−3** to the careless caster (guilt) | the friendly-fire damage sites in `resolveHeroAction` |
| **Big hit taken** (a hit ≥ 30% of your maxHp in one blow) | **−5** | `recordHitTaken`, gated on fraction |
| **Talked down** (a hostile banter beat — boastful rival, taunting enemy) | **−4** | banter beat hook (§ below) |
| **Lost a bet** (gambling shrine) | **−6** | shrine resolution |
| **God's wrath** (offended deity / failed offering) | **−12** (superstitious ×2) | religion event (§9) |
| **Outmatched** (an engaged enemy is ≥ +3 levels above you, sustained) | **−1 / 5s** while it persists | `partyLogic`, mirrors the `enemyLevelAbove` trigger |
| **Starving / no potions when low** | **−2 / 10s** while below 25% HP with zero hp-potions | drip, models desperation |
| **Permadeath of a roster-mate** (a friend permadied this run) | **−8** to survivors | `killHero`/wipe sites |

**The cheer/jeer "banter beat" hook (the crucial sim/app handshake).** The prompt wants "allies cheering or complimenting each other" to raise morale and "being talked down" to lower it — but banter is app-side. Resolution: the *sim* periodically (deterministically, every `MORALE_BEAT_TICKS = 100` ticks ≈ 10s, using `rng.combat`) selects whether a **social beat** occurs and of what *valence* (cheer/jeer), keyed off party composition and current morale spread (e.g., the highest-morale hero tends to cheer the lowest; a boastful hero near a rival tends to jeer). It emits `{t:'moraleBeat', from, to, valence}` and applies the ±5/−4 via `applyMorale`. The **banter director then reads that event** and picks the *words* — a cheerful bard's "Chin up, we've got this!" or a deadpan "Statistically, we're fine." The mechanical effect is sim-deterministic; the dialogue is app-side flavor. This is the inversion the prompt demands: **banter is the consequence of a morale event, never its cause.** Heroes who never speak (the mime) still emit and receive beats — the mime "cheers" via a silent stage direction app-side, but the +5 is real.

## 8. Recovery between (and within) runs

- **Within a run:** the only upward drift mid-crawl is the calm-regroup +1/10s (capped) and social cheer beats. Morale earned/lost in the dungeon is *sticky* — that's the point. A great run sends heroes home Inspired; a brutal one sends them home Shaken.

- **Between runs (village):** morale decays **toward the hero's trait-adjusted baseline** (usually 0, ±10 for gloomy/cheerful) on wall-clock time, folded into the existing `villageUpkeep` accrual path — the one analytic function that already handles HP/MP recovery and works offline. Add a `MORALE_RECOVERY_PER_SEC` term:

  ```
  drift = MORALE_RECOVERY_PER_SEC * dtSec               // e.g. 0.5/sec → ~33 pts/min
  hero.morale → move toward moraleBaseline(hero) by min(drift, |gap|)
  ```

  This rides the same `dtMs`/`OFFLINE_CAP_MS` clamp, so a hero who comes home routed (−70) is roughly back to neutral after a few minutes of village time, and an overnight-closed tab caps the same way HP does. No 4×-idle exploit (it's wall-clock, never tick-scaled).

- **Hospital / Tavern hooks (future content):** a "buy a round at the Tavern" action could instantly nudge the whole roster +morale for gold — a natural village economy lever, and an obvious place for the chef/bard's support identity. Left as a content/command extension (`{t:'TAVERN_ROUND'}`), not required for v1.

- **A hero who comes home below −40 enters a `'recovering'` activity** even at full HP (they need to *rest their nerves*), gating them out of the next embark for a beat — a gentle, legible consequence that makes morale matter to the roster manager without a punishing death spiral.

## 9. Races & religion

Neither exists in the codebase yet, so morale is designed as the **first consumer** of two thin new content axes, both JSON-extensible and seeded-RNG-friendly (consistent with the project's "deeply JSON-extensible / ADOM-rich" values). Each is optional: a hero with no race/religion is morale-neutral.

**Races** (`content/races/*.json`, each a `RaceDef { id, name, mods, moraleProfile }`). `moraleProfile` is the same shape as the trait table — baseline shift, gain/loss mults, band shifts. Examples that write themselves:
- **Dwarf** — `lossMult 0.7`, `bandShift −10` (stubborn, hard to rout). The reliable front line.
- **Halfling** — `baseline +8`, gains extra from *looting* and *meals* (ties to the `hungry` trait). Plucky.
- **Half-orc** — `gainMult 1.5` on kills, but `lossMult 1.3` (volatile). Pairs explosively with bloodthirsty.
- **Elf** — `bandShift +5` toward composure, but extra loss from *witnessing death* (long memory). 
- **Undead/Revenant** (graveyard tie-in) — *immune to morale*, always Steady. The unflappable option for players who hate managing it.

Race plugs into `moraleBand`/`moraleBaseline`/the delta funnel exactly like traits; a hero's race and traits **stack multiplicatively**.

**Religion** (`content/religions/*.json`, `ReligionDef { id, name, deity, domain, moraleHooks }`). Religion is morale's most *eventful* interaction because deities react to play:
- **Offerings & favor:** a hero can tithe gold at a **village Shrine** (new building / command `{t:'MAKE_OFFERING'}`) for a deterministic favor roll on `rng.village`; success grants a temporary party-wide morale floor for the next run ("blessed: cannot drop below Shaken"), failure or stinginess risks **god's wrath**.
- **Domain alignment in the dungeon:** a deity of *War* grants its faithful +morale-per-kill; a deity of *Mercy* grants +morale on *rescues* and a penalty for slaying neutrals (synergizes with the existing "the party feels bad about the chicken" chronicle line — killing the lost kitten could actually *anger* a Mercy god, −morale to the worshipper). All deterministic: domain hooks are just more `source`-keyed multipliers and a few new emit sites.
- **God's wrath** is the prompt's named loss source: a scripted event (deterministic, `rng.combat`-rolled when worship conditions are violated — e.g., a War-god worshipper *fleeing* a winnable fight) that applies the −12 and a brief debuff status. **superstitious** heroes take double and are the most religion-reactive — the trait, race, and religion axes all converge on the same morale funnel.
- **Gambling shrine / bets:** the "losing a bet" source is religion-flavored content (a trickster deity's dice game) — a command-driven village minigame whose payout/penalty is a seeded roll, feeding ±6 morale through the same funnel.

Crucially, *all three axes (traits, race, religion) are pure data tables read by the same four pure functions* (`moraleBand`, `moraleBaseline`, `moraleCombatMods` via band, and the `applyMorale` delta funnel). Adding a race or religion is a JSON file plus a `gen-content` validation pass — no sim code changes. That is the project's content-pipeline ethos applied to morale.

## 10. Determinism, purity, and the golden re-baseline

- **Purity:** every morale read/write is inside `src/game`, draws only from `state.rng.combat`/`rng.village`, and the new event flows out to the app. `src/game/purity.test.ts` stays green — no `Math.random`, no clock except the existing `nowMs` recovery path.
- **RNG stream discipline (the one thing to get right):** the only *new* dice are **dodge** (Confident+) and **freeze/social-beat** (Shaken / periodic). Each is drawn **conditionally**, only when a hero is actually in the relevant band — so any seed whose party never leaves the Steady band consumes the *identical* rng stream it does today. This means the golden re-baseline is **expected and bounded**: morale changes the golden hash only on seeds where a hero crosses ±25, exactly the "deliberate sim change → re-baseline in the same commit" path `determinism.test.ts` documents. Re-run the snippet, update the two constants, commit together.
- **Save/load:** `morale` is a plain integer on `Hero`, so `serialize`/`deserialize` round-trip it for free; the save-equivalence half of the determinism test holds without changes.
- **Balance harness:** after wiring, run `bun run balance`. Watch that panic doesn't manufacture `timeout` rows (Section 4's guardrails — panic never deadlocks travel and never single-handedly aborts a floor — are specifically there to protect that invariant). Expect a *slightly* swingier outcome mix (a few more `wiped` when a key hero routs at a bad moment, a few more deep `withdrawn` when an Inspired party snowballs) — which is the design delivering drama. Re-baseline the goldens in the same commit.

## 11. One-paragraph summary of the data flow (the thing that must be true)

A monster dies → `processDeaths` (sim) calls `applyMorale(killer, +3, 'kill')` and broadcasts `+1` to witnesses → each call clamps, mutates `hero.morale`, and pushes a `{t:'morale'}` event → next tick, `derive()` and the resolvers read `hero.morale` through `moraleBand`/`moraleCombatMods` so the now-Confident killer crits a touch more and the tactics evaluator may unlock their `selfMoraleAbove` rule → meanwhile, app-side, `BanterDirector.consume` sees the `{t:'morale'}` event and *maybe* makes the bard say "That's the spirit!" — words that change nothing in `GameState`. The sim is the source of truth; banter is the weather report, never the weather.

# Religion & gods
**Favor model.** FAVOR is a per-hero integer in [-100, +100] stored on the Hero as `faith: { religionId: ReligionId | null; favor: number; streakTicks: number; lastWrathTick: number } | null`. Non-believers have `faith: null`. Favor starts at 0 on conversion (at the Temple, a new village building) and is clamped to [-100,+100] after every change.

FAVOR TIERS drive all blessings/wrath (checked when favor crosses a boundary, and rolled against on the relevant trigger):
  Forsaken [-100..-61], Scorned [-60..-21], Wary [-20..+20], Faithful [+21..+60], Exalted [+61..+100].

HOW FAVOR CHANGES — all deltas are deterministic integers applied at tick boundaries (dungeon) or on village commands; none use wall-clock:
  +DEVOTIONS (per-religion `devotions[]`, each keyed to an event the sim already emits — monsterDied of a matching family, chestGold>=N, heal cast, hero leveled): each grants a fixed favor delta (typ +1..+5) the tick the event fires. A `streakTicks` field caps repeat-spam (same devotion grants favor at most once per 30 ticks = 3s).
  -TABOOS (per-religion `taboos[]`): violating one applies a fixed penalty (typ -3..-20) the tick the forbidden event fires (e.g. a monsterDied event whose defId is `chicken` for the Poultry god). Some taboos are passive caps (hospital heal cap) rather than deltas.
  PASSIVE DRIFT: while the believing hero is alive in the party, favor drifts toward `restingFavor` (default 0) by 1 point every 600 ticks (60s) in the dungeon, and 1 point per 5 min wall-clock in the village (computed from nowMs deltas, never read live). Prevents permanent parking at the extremes without tending.
  TITHING: at the Temple the player donates gold for favor at religion-specific rates (typ 10 gold => +1 favor, capped +20/day via a purchasedToday-style key). The reverse never costs gold.

WHEN BLESSINGS FIRE: each `blessings[]` entry has a required `tier` (usually Exalted, some Faithful) and a `roll` — chance(state.rng.combat, p) (or .loot/.village for economy ones) on its trigger. Signature: DIVINE INTERVENTION — when an Exalted hero would be Downed, before the down resolves, roll chance(rng.combat, p) where p scales 0.04 at +61 up to ~0.12 at +100 (linear p = 0.04 + (favor-61)/39*0.08; death god is higher). On success the hero is restored to a religion-defined maxHp fraction (typ 0.5), gets a divineIntervention event + a buff status, and favor is spent (-25). Because it costs favor it cannot chain — the rare sudden resurrection.

WHEN WRATH FIRES: each `wrath[]` entry requires a low tier (Scorned/Forsaken) and rolls on its trigger. Minor wrath (Scorned) is per-tick/per-event nuisance (botched heals, soured loot, a `cursed` status). Signature: MANIFESTATION — while ANY party hero is Forsaken (favor <= -61), every 200 ticks (20s) roll chance(rng.combat, p) with p = 0.05 + (-61 - favor)/39*0.15 (up to 0.20 at -100). On success the god MANIFESTS: an `avatar_<religionId>` boss-statted MonsterInstance (spawned via rng.combat) appears adjacent to the offender and attacks the WHOLE party until slain or the party withdraws; killing it sets the offender to favor 0 ('the debt is paid') and emits avatarSlain. `lastWrathTick` throttles to one live avatar per run.

DETERMINISM: every roll draws from an existing GameState.rng stream (combat for dungeon resurrection/manifestation/cursed rolls, loot for soured/blessed drops, village for tithe/temple/recovery rolls). No Math.random/Date.now. Favor deltas, drift, and tier transitions are pure integer math on serialized fields, so the golden-hash determinism test covers them; adding the system requires re-baselining the two golden constants in determinism.test.ts in the same commit. New content lives as JSON under content/religions/*.json compiled by tools/gen-content.ts to src/game/content/gen/religions.gen.ts; cross-refs (avatar monster defId, taboo monster families, devotion event kinds) are validated at gen time exactly like monster drop/item refs.

**Non-believer (Faithless).** The Faithless / 'I'll believe it when a god pays my bar tab.' A hero with faith=null worships no one, owes no one, and fears no manifestation. The default state for every recruit; conversion happens only at the Temple and is reversible (apostasy) for a gold fee and a one-time -10 favor scar on any future religion.
- Bonuses: No taboos: never loses favor, never suffers wrath, never has a god manifest to kill the party. Total mechanical freedom — kill every chicken, take every medicine, hoard every coin.; +5% XP gain (mult xp 1.05): unburdened by ritual, they just grind. Represents the opportunity cost the faithful pay in devotions/taboos.; Full hospital healing and full natural recovery (no faith-based caps).; Immune to the 'apostate' penalty and to rival-god jealousy effects that can hit believers carrying the wrong relic.; Can freely use ALL consumables, relics, and altars as neutral loot (e.g. drink from any shrine font for its raw effect, no devotion/taboo strings).
- Drawbacks: No blessings: cannot be divinely resurrected. A Downed Faithless hero is just Downed — permadeath odds on wipe are unchanged with no second chance.; No favor-gated stat perks, no blessed loot tables, no avatar-slaying favor windfalls.; Shrines and altars found in the dungeon give a Faithless hero only the small generic font effect, never the large aligned-faith boon.; Slightly worse village standing: NPC priests give no faith discount on hospital or resurrection-adjacent services.; Cannot benefit from class-religion synergies (necromancer + death god, paladin + war/sun god, chef + glutton god) that meaningfully boost those builds.

# Races
**Trait model.** EVERY HERO ENDS WITH 2-5 TRAITS = innate race traits (1-2, fixed by race) + rolled personality traits (1-3, from the existing TRAIT_IDS pool). The split: a hero always carries its race's `racialTraits` (these are permanent, never rerolled, and live alongside `perks`/`injuries` — they emit StatMods through derive() exactly like perks, plus a few bespoke flags handled in the sim like injuries color_blind/fear_of_chickens do). On TOP of that, `rollTraits(rng)` picks personality traits as today, but with two changes: (1) count is now 1 + (roll<60?1:0) + (roll<25?1:0) so a hero rolls 1-3 personality traits, and (2) the pool is BIASED by race.

DATA WIRING (concrete, deterministic):
- Add `raceId: string` to Hero. makeHero rolls it via pickWeighted(state.rng.village, RACES, r => r.rarity) BEFORE rolling personality traits and name, so every roll stays on the single village stream and golden-hash stays reproducible (re-baseline once when this lands).
- Apply statMods/hpMod/speedMod by appending the race's racialTraits ids into a new derive() pass: derive() already loops perks/injuries/statuses for StatMods — add `for (const rt of raceTraits(hero.raceId)) mods.push(...rt.mods)`. statMods become {kind:'attr',...} entries, hpMod becomes {kind:'mult',what:'maxhp',mul:1+hpMod/100}, speedMod becomes {kind:'mult',what:'speed',mul:1+speedMod/100}. So no new math path — races reuse the existing StatMod engine. The statMods/hpMod/speedMod fields below are the SOURCE the gen step compiles into those StatMods; bespoke racialTrait effects (regen, fear, telepathy, etc.) are named hooks the sim reads, mirroring how injuries color_blind is special-cased.
- Zero-sum check: each race's statMods sum to roughly 0 (signature + plus a matching minus), with hpMod/speedMod as the secondary lever. Net "power" is balanced so race is flavor+texture, not a strict upgrade; rarity (spawn weight) is the only thing that makes weird races rare, NOT power.

PERSONALITY-TRAIT BIAS (how races tilt the rolled pool): rollTraits builds a weight map over TRAIT_IDS (boastful, gloomy, cheerful, greedy, cowardly, poetic, deadpan, bloodthirsty, superstitious, hungry, motherly, scholarly), default weight 10 each. The race contributes a per-trait multiplier so e.g. a Halfling triples 'hungry' and 'cheerful' and halves 'bloodthirsty'; a Goblin triples 'greedy'/'cowardly'; a Skeleton (reformed) triples 'deadpan'/'poetic' and zeroes 'hungry' (no stomach). pickWeighted draws from this biased map WITHOUT replacement until the rolled count is filled. Because every weight is >0 (we clamp to a floor of 1 unless a race hard-zeroes a trait for canon reasons), any combo is still possible — race only shifts odds, so the determinism stays a pure function of (race, rng). The per-race bias lists live in the `quirks`/restrictions text below as design intent; the implementable form is a `personalityBias: Record<TraitId, number>` map per race that the gen step emits next to racialTraits. Result: a party reads as a mix — race sets the floor of identity (2 fixed-feeling slots from 1-2 racial traits) and personality fills the rest, giving the 2-5 total with strong flavor coherence (a Myconid Druid almost always ends up poetic+scholarly; a Half-Orc Barbarian almost always bloodthirsty+boastful) while never being fully predictable.

RESTRICTIONS MODEL: `restrictions` are sim-readable tags resolved against existing class fields. 'noMetalArmor' intersects armorKinds against {mail,plate,shield} and drops them (like the barbarian already has armorKinds:[]). 'noClassId:necromancer' blocks that race from rolling/being hired into a class at the tavern (gen validates the id exists). 'speakBanterOnly' / 'silentBanter' reuse the mime's never-speaks pipeline. Others ('cannotBeHealedBySoup','immuneToPoison','wrongPotionChance:0.1') are named flags the relevant sim system checks, exactly like injuries already do. Nothing here needs a new randomness source — all rolls (race pick, personality pick, any bespoke proc like ghost phase-through or goblin flee) draw from the appropriate seeded stream (village for creation, combat for in-fight procs).

# Dungeon events
**Interaction model.** DETERMINISTIC, IDLE-SAFE EVENT MODEL — fully expressible in the existing src/game sim.

== Where events fire ==
A new system `dungeonEvents(state, run, nowMs, events)` runs inside advanceTick, slotted AFTER partyLogic's room-discovery block and BEFORE the descend/withdraw checks (so an event can't fire on the same tick the party leaves the floor). It only fires while `run.mode==='dungeon'`, `run.intent==='explore'`, no hero is downed, and no event is already pending.

== Triggering (seeded, no wall-clock) ==
Trigger on entering a freshly-visited room (reuse partyLogic's `run.visitedRooms.push(ri)` branch). For each newly entered room index `ri`, roll with an EPHEMERAL rng `r = makeRng(hash2(run.runSeed, 0xE7E7 ^ (run.depth*131 + ri)))` so an event is a pure function of (seed, depth, room) — re-deriving never double-fires. First roll `chance(r, EVENT_ROOM_CHANCE=0.22)`; if it hits, build the eligible pool = events whose `trigger` band matches `run.depth` and whose floor-feature precondition holds (e.g. "needs an unopened chest in this room", "needs a free floor tile"), then `pickWeighted(r, pool, e => e.rarity)`. The chosen event's per-outcome rolls also draw from `state.rng.combat`/`state.rng.loot` at resolution time, exactly like combat/chest loot already do — so outcomes stay on the same canonical streams the determinism golden already pins.

== New serializable state (added to DungeonRun) ==
`run.pendingEvent?: { eventId: string; roomIndex: number; choiceKeys: string[]; defaultChoice: string; deadlineTick: number; rolledSeed: number }` — plain JSON. When set, the sim PAUSES party objective travel for that tick onward (in heroDecisions, if `run.pendingEvent` exists, heroes hold position: they still defend/heal/finish in-flight actions and the player may still WITHDRAW, but `computeObjective` travel is skipped). This is the "pause the auto-crawl" moment. Also `run.favor` (int, religion standing, starts 0), `run.morale` (int 0–100, starts 50), each a single number, fully serializable; race lives on the hero as an optional `hero.race` string (defaults 'human').

== The prompt / modal ==
On set, push a transient `{ t:'eventOpened', eventId, prompt, choices:[{key,label}] }` GameEvent. The UI (read-only) renders a modal with the flavor `prompt` and 2–3 buttons. Buttons dispatch a Command `{ t:'EVENT_CHOICE'; eventId; choiceKey }`, applied at the next tick boundary like every other command. applyCommand validates the choiceKey is in `pendingEvent.choiceKeys` and that `eventId` matches (stale clicks are ignored), then resolves the chosen outcome via weighted `rand(state.rng.combat)` over that choice's `outcomes`, applies the concrete result (gold/items/statuses/spawn/etc. through the SAME helpers combat already uses: rollChestLoot, spawn a MonsterInstance, push statuses, addXp, adjust run.favor/morale), pushes an `{ t:'eventResolved', eventId, choiceKey, resultText }` event, and clears `run.pendingEvent`.

== Idle auto-resolve (never softlocks) ==
`pendingEvent.deadlineTick = state.tick + EVENT_TIMEOUT_TICKS` (300 ticks = 30s at 10 tps). Each tick, dungeonEvents checks `state.tick >= deadlineTick`; if reached with no player command, it auto-resolves the `defaultChoice` (the cautious/leave-it-alone option, e.g. "Walk away", "Don't touch it") through the identical resolution path. Because the player being AWAY just means the default fires deterministically at a fixed tick, two replays of the same seed produce identical results whether or not the human ever clicked — and the crawl always resumes. If the player clicks before the deadline, that choice fires instead; the deadline is then moot. Withdrawing while an event is open auto-resolves the default immediately then proceeds home.

== Determinism guarantees ==
(1) eligibility/selection uses an ephemeral hash2 rng keyed by (runSeed, depth, room) — never wall-clock; (2) outcome rolls use the canonical combat/loot streams at the tick the choice is applied; (3) the player's choice enters as a Command at a tick boundary, so its ordering is fixed; (4) auto-resolve fires at an absolute tick, so an idle replay == an interactive replay of the same clicks. Re-baseline the determinism golden once after wiring this in (expected change, per CLAUDE.md). All numbers below are concrete and scale via existing balance helpers (monsterMaxHp/monsterXp at run.depth, rollChestLoot(state, run.depth), POTION amount ~10, gold in the tens-to-hundreds band matching monsterGoldMult).

# Gambler's Cove
### Dredge Dice (`game_dredge_dice`)
The classic cove warm-up. The croupier (a kobold named Pip) rolls two seeded d6 from rng.village; the hero rolls two more. High total wins; ties go to the house. A 'snake-eyes' bonus (hero rolls 2) pays triple but a 'boxcars' bust (hero rolls 12) loses double the stake. Fast, swingy, and the gateway drug of the cove.
- Odds: ~46.3% hero win (ties to house), so house edge ~7.4% on a flat win/loss before special rolls. Snake-eyes (hero rolls exactly 2): p=1/36, pays 3x net. Boxcars (hero rolls exactly 12): p=1/36, loses 2x stake. Implementation: roll4 = randInt(rng,1,6) x4 from rng.village; resolve as above. Net expected return ~0.91x per gold staked. · Bets: 5-50 gold (cap rises to 200 once maxDepthReached >= 5)

### Skullcup (`game_skullcup`)
A push-your-luck cup game. The hero keeps flipping a seeded coin; each heads doubles the pot, the first tails ends the round and pays out whatever the pot reached BELOW the cash-out line. The hero's greed level (driven by the greedy trait or addiction stage) sets how many flips they auto-attempt before banking. Watch a cautious cleric bank at 2 flips and a greedy barbarian ride it to zero.
- Odds: Each flip: 50% heads (continue, pot x2), 50% tails (bust to 0 unless already banked). Hero auto-banks after N flips where N = 1 + greedTier (greedy trait or addiction stage adds tiers, capped at 4). Expected value of riding to N flips before a forced bank = stake * (0.5^N) * (2^N) minus bust paths; tuned so banking at 2 = ~1.0x EV (fair), 3 = ~0.95x, 4 = ~0.85x (greed is punished). All flips from rng.village. · Bets: 10-40 gold (the cup has a fixed table limit, never scales — keeps the addiction floor low)

### The Wheel of Woe (`game_wheel_of_woe`)
A creaking 12-segment wheel painted with tiny disasters and jackpots. One spin from rng.village. Most segments pay 0-1.5x, two segments pay big (5x and the legendary 12x 'Jackpot of Woe'), and one segment is 'The Curse' — you lose your stake AND the cove keeps your worst unequipped junk item as a souvenir (flavor only; picks the lowest-value inventory item).
- Odds: 12 equal segments (each p=1/12): 4x 'nothing' (0x), 3x 'consolation' (1.0x return), 2x 'tidy' (1.5x), 1x 'lucky' (3x), 1x 'big' (5x), 1x 'JACKPOT' (12x). Expected return = (4*0 + 3*1 + 2*1.5 + 3 + 5 + 12)/12 = 26/12 ≈ 0.92x net before the curse flavor. Segment chosen via randInt(rng.village,0,11). · Bets: 20-100 gold (scales to 400 once a boss is slain — high rollers only)

### Liar's Loot (`game_liars_loot`)
A bluffing card-draw against the house dealer. Both draw a seeded card 1-13; before reveal the hero may 'call' (their tactics/trait decide based on their drawn card) — calling on a high card pays 2x, folding returns half the stake. The deadpan and scholarly traits fold smartly; boastful and greedy heroes overcall and bleed gold. A pure skill-flavored game where the hero's PERSONALITY is the strategy.
- Odds: Hero card vs dealer card, both randInt(rng.village,1,13). Hero auto-strategy by trait: smart play (call iff card>=8) wins ~0.96x EV; reckless play (call iff card>=4, boastful/greedy) wins ~0.80x EV; timid play (fold below 11, cowardly) ~0.88x EV. House wins ties. Calling pays 2x on win / loses stake on loss; folding returns 0.5x stake. · Bets: 15-75 gold

### The Rat Race (`game_rat_race`)
Six trained dungeon rats (Nibbles, Cheddar, Sir Squeaks, Plague, Biscuit, and The Undefeated) race a tiny track. The hero bets on one rat to win. Odds are posted per rat and seeded — 'The Undefeated' is the heavy favorite and pays little; long-shot Plague pays huge but almost never wins. A whole side-spectacle other idle heroes can 'watch' and banter about.
- Odds: Winner chosen by pickWeighted(rng.village) over rats with win-weights [Undefeated 30, Cheddar 22, Biscuit 18, Sir Squeaks 14, Nibbles 11, Plague 5] (sum 100). Posted payouts (with house cut baked in): Undefeated 2.6x, Cheddar 3.5x, Biscuit 4.2x, Sir Squeaks 5.4x, Nibbles 6.8x, Plague 14x. Every payout's (winChance * payout) lands at 0.78-0.92x — the long shot is the worst EV, as it should be. · Bets: 10-60 gold

WHILE THE PLAYER IDLES IN THE VILLAGE: every accrual tick (reuse the existing wall-clock chore-accrual cadence, ~ once per real minute, entering as nowMs), each idle 'addicted' hero rolls from rng.village to maybe auto-gamble their OWN hero-specific gold (this assumes the new hero-specific gold model). All randomness via rand(rng.village)/randInt — never wall-clock or the built-in random fn. PER-TICK FLOW: (1) roll chance to gamble = 0.20 * stageMult (curious 1x, hooked 1.5x, degenerate 2x) — so 20-40% of idle minutes; if the hero has < MIN_WALLET (10) personal gold, they never gamble (they're broke, they pout in banter instead). (2) If gambling, pick a game weighted toward cheap ones (Dredge Dice/Skullcup favored), compute a stake = clamp(round(personalGold * BET_FRACTION), gameMin, gameMax) where BET_FRACTION = 0.10 (curious) / 0.15 (hooked) / 0.20 (degenerate) — they bet a SLICE, never the whole wallet. (3) Resolve the game against rng.village exactly as a manual play; win or lose adjusts ONLY this hero's personal gold. BOUNDS THAT KEEP IT FUNNY, NOT RUINOUS: (a) PER-TICK STAKE CAP = min(20% of wallet, 50 gold) hard cap regardless of stage — one spin can never nuke them. (b) PER-DAY LOSS FLOOR: an addicted hero will NOT auto-gamble below RESERVE = 25 gold of personal wealth; the cove bouncer (a polite ogre) cuts them off, emitting a funny chronicle line ('Grit was escorted out of the Gambler's Cove, again'). So they can lose at most (wallet - 25) per day, and only a slice per tick. (c) STAGE PROGRESSION is gated on NET lifetime cove losses, not raw plays, and only advances one stage per day max — nobody spirals to 'degenerate' overnight. (d) HOUSE EDGE is gentle (~8% per the games above) so expected daily drift is small: a degenerate idle hero with 200 gold loses on average ~6-12 gold per idle hour, not their shirt. (e) WINS HAPPEN: the seeded streams mean an addicted hero sometimes walks away RICH, and the banter/chronicle celebrates it ('Brann hit the Jackpot of Woe and bought everyone potions') — the variance is the fun. (f) OPT-OUT: assigning the hero to a CHORE or the dungeon party makes activity != 'idle', so they don't gamble; the player can always pull a problem gambler off the floor. (g) DETERMINISM: because every roll is rng.village + nowMs-driven accrual, the whole thing replays identically on save/load and is covered by the golden-hash test.

# New classes
**Summon reconciliation.** Three "summon" identities that previously overlapped are now cleanly separated by RESOURCE, SOURCE, and FANTASY, so each plays differently in the tactics system:

1) NECROMANCER — REANIMATES WHAT IT KILLS (resource: CORPSES). Source = the specific enemy bodies the party drops near it. Throttle = the floor-local corpse pool (must kill first) PLUS a hard level-budget of 2x hero level in total undead-levels. You don't choose what you raise so much as what you killed — raise an ogre, you get an ogre. Reactive, momentum-based, snowballs off a fight in progress, floor-scoped (pool clears on descend). Fantasy: a battlefield grows your army. The old generic 'raise_skeleton' minion-spawn is REMOVED to make room — skeletons are no longer the Necromancer's thing; risen ENEMIES are.

2) SUMMONER — CONJURES FROM A SPELLBOOK (resource: MANA/MP). Source = learnable summon spells bought at the Magician's Guild (imps, golem, spirit wolves, phoenix), each yielding a FIXED, known minion regardless of the battlefield. Throttle = MP cost + a menagerie cap of 1.5x hero level in minion-levels + cooldowns; mana regen is the pace-setter, never corpses. Proactive, sustained, you pick your warband composition in advance. Fantasy: you bring your own menagerie. This is the class that "learns a variety of summon spells."

3) BEEKEEPER (existing) — stays exactly as-is: 'release_the_bees' is a flavor summon-buff (3 short-lived bees from the hive), not a pet-management identity. It remains a SUPPORT class whose summon is a cooldown ability, not a budget/resource system — so it doesn't compete with the two dedicated summon classes.

Net effect on the data: the Necromancer's skillUnlocks swap 'raise_skeleton' → 'reanimate_fallen' (+ adds 'corpse_explosion'); 'skeleton_minion' can be retired or repurposed as a generic 'risen_undead' fallback def. The Summoner is a brand-new caster class with its own 'imp_minion', 'stone_golem_minion', 'spirit_wolf_minion' defs (marked summonOnly: true in content). All three respect the purity rule: every minion choice, target pick, variant roll, and tie-break draws from rng.combat / rng.loot seeded streams — never wall-clock, never the language-builtin random.

**Necromancer rework.** RAISE THE FALLEN (corpse-budget reanimation, replacing the old raise_skeleton minion-conjure). The Necromancer no longer pulls generic skeletons out of the floor — it reanimates the SPECIFIC enemies the party has just killed, and the strength of what you raise is bounded by your level.

LEVEL BUDGET: max simultaneous undead = 2x hero level, measured in UNDEAD-LEVELS. An L4 Necromancer commands up to 8 undead-levels of risen dead — that is eight L1 zombies, OR two reanimated L4 ogres, OR a L3 wraith + a L2 ghoul + three L1 rats, in any combination. Raising past 8 is refused (the skill won't fire in tactics). The budget scales every level-up, so a deep-run Necromancer fields a genuine horde.

THE CORPSE POOL: every enemy the party kills near the Necromancer drops a CORPSE token into a floor-local pool (capacity ~6 corpses, FIFO; a killed L5 ogre is a "L5 corpse"). The reanimation skill, 'Reanimate the Fallen' (replaces raise_skeleton; target self, range 0, mpCost 20, cooldown 150 ticks, castTicks 12), consumes corpses from the pool: it deterministically reanimates the HIGHEST-level corpses that still fit under the remaining budget (ties broken by FIFO order — no wall-clock, no language-builtin random). Each reanimated unit is a 'risen_<sourceDefId>' minion with ~60% of the original's stats, summonedBy the Necromancer, that fights for the party until it falls apart (durationTicks 400) or is re-killed. No corpses in the pool = the skill cannot fire; this makes the Necromancer a momentum class that needs early fodder kills before it snowballs into an army of its own enemies. Corpses do NOT persist between floors (the pool clears on descend), keeping it deterministic and floor-scoped like the existing ephemeral-rng floor model.

The Necromancer keeps soul_bolt (its INT-scaling poke) so it isn't helpless before the first kill, and gains a new L5 capstone 'Corpse Explosion' (target enemiesInRadius radius 2; detonates one active risen minion for magical AoE = 40% of that minion's max HP, spending its undead-level back to the budget) — turning the horde into ammunition. Stats, sprite, weapon/armor kinds are unchanged from the existing necromancer entry; only the summon mechanic is reworked from generic-skeleton-conjure to kill-the-thing-then-raise-the-thing.

### Priest (`priest`) — healer
A divine healer/buffer who channels a chosen deity. Where the Cleric heals by rote and 'quietly judges life choices,' the Priest is genuinely, fervently devout, and the dungeon answers. Their power waxes and wanes with FAITH, a resource that builds by doing the deity's bidding (healing allies, smiting the unclean) and is spent on miracles too big for mere MP. Deeply tied to the new religion system: at the village Temple a Priest swears to one of three deities, and that vow re-skins their kit (the radiant Sun, the merciful Moon, the stern Forge), all selected from seeded village rng — never wall-clock.
- Base: STR 4 DEX 3 INT 9 VIT 7
- Signature: FAITH METER (0-100, separate from MP). Faith DECAYS by 1 every 30 ticks out of combat so it can't be banked forever. At >=70 Faith the Priest enters STATE OF GRACE: all their heals and buffs gain +25% power and cost 0 MP for as long as Faith stays >=70. 'Divine Intervention' (their capstone) consumes 70 Faith to fire. All Faith rolls/thresholds are deterministic integer accumulation; any tie-breaking (which ally to res, etc.) draws from rng.combat.

### Gambler (`gambler`) — melee_dps

Tied to the new COVE (the seaside gambling den): the Gambler buys Loaded Dice and marked cards there, and their whole identity is high-variance risk/reward — coin-flip skills that either crit gloriously or whiff, with mechanics to nudge the odds in their favor.
